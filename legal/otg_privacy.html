2. Information We Collect

OTG Analytics is designed to collect only the information reasonably necessary to operate, secure, understand, and improve the platform.

Depending on how you access the site, we may process:

  • a randomly generated analytics session identifier;
  • a pseudonymous visitor identifier;
  • browser and device category;
  • browser family;
  • language or locale;
  • timezone;
  • page or application mode entered;
  • selected item identifier where relevant;
  • referring website domain;
  • UTM campaign parameters;
  • timestamps;
  • bot or automated-traffic indicators; and
  • indicators used to separate internal or testing traffic from ordinary visitor activity.

3. IP Address and Pseudonymous Visitor Identifier

Our server may receive your IP address as part of the normal technical process required to establish an internet connection.

OTG Analytics does not intentionally store the raw client IP address in its visitor analytics database.

Instead, where visitor estimation is performed, the platform generates a pseudonymous identifier using a keyed cryptographic HMAC based on limited connection and browser characteristics.

The resulting identifier is designed to help estimate repeat usage without storing the raw IP address.

This identifier is pseudonymous and should not be considered fully anonymous. Network changes, VPNs, shared connections, browser changes, and multiple devices can also affect its accuracy.

4. Cookies

The current first-party visitor analytics implementation does not set a persistent analytics cookie for the purpose of identifying visitors across sessions.

The platform may store a randomly generated first-party browser identifier in local browser storage for pseudonymous analytics continuity. The raw identifier is not stored in the analytics database; server-side analytics uses a pseudonymous cryptographic digest.

Some technical cookies or browser storage may nevertheless be used by underlying application technology where necessary for operation of the service.

If OTG Analytics later introduces persistent analytics, authentication, account, preference, or marketing cookies, this Privacy Notice and any required consent mechanism will be updated before those technologies are used.

5. Why We Process This Information

We may process the information described above to:

  • operate and maintain OTG Analytics;
  • understand how the platform is used;
  • measure aggregate website traffic;
  • estimate unique and returning visitors;
  • measure daily, weekly, and monthly platform activity;
  • understand which analytics sections are used;
  • evaluate marketing campaigns through UTM parameters;
  • understand general traffic sources;
  • distinguish ordinary visitors from bots and internal testing;
  • detect technical problems and abuse;
  • improve performance and usability;
  • plan future features; and
  • produce aggregated platform-growth statistics for internal analysis, partners, or potential investors.

We do not use visitor analytics to make decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects concerning individual users.

6. Legal Basis

Where the General Data Protection Regulation or similar legislation applies, OTG Analytics processes limited server-side technical and usage information on the basis of legitimate interests in:

  • operating the service;
  • maintaining security;
  • preventing abuse;
  • understanding aggregate usage;
  • measuring platform performance; and
  • improving the product.

These interests are pursued using data-minimisation measures, including avoiding storage of raw IP addresses in the analytics database and limiting the information collected.

Where another legal basis is required by applicable law for a particular future processing activity, that processing will not be introduced without the appropriate legal basis and disclosures.

7. Traffic Sources and Campaign Information

If you visit OTG Analytics through a tagged marketing link, we may record standard campaign parameters such as:

  • utm_source;
  • utm_medium;
  • utm_campaign;
  • utm_content; and
  • utm_term.

We may also record the hostname of the referring website.

We do not intentionally store the complete referring URL where the hostname alone is sufficient for analytics.

8. What We Do Not Intentionally Collect for Visitor Analytics

The current analytics system is not designed to store:

  • raw client IP addresses;
  • passwords;
  • wallet private keys;
  • seed phrases;
  • complete browser cookies;
  • complete request headers;
  • complete referring URLs;
  • payment-card information; or
  • sensitive personal information.

Never send a private key, seed phrase, password, or other authentication secret to OTG Analytics.

9. Sharing of Information

We do not sell personal data to advertisers.

Information may be processed by infrastructure or hosting providers where necessary to operate the website and its database.

We may also disclose information where reasonably necessary to:

  • comply with applicable law;
  • respond to lawful requests;
  • protect the security or integrity of the service; or
  • investigate fraud, abuse, or attacks.

Statistics shared publicly or with potential partners or investors should, where reasonably possible, be presented in aggregated form rather than as individual visitor records.

10. International Data Transfers

Depending on the infrastructure and service providers used to operate OTG Analytics, personal data may be processed in countries outside your country of residence.

Where applicable law requires safeguards for international transfers, appropriate transfer mechanisms will be used.

11. Data Retention

Raw visitor analytics session records are retained only for as long as reasonably necessary for platform analytics, security, troubleshooting, and product-development purposes.

Retention periods may vary depending on operational requirements. OTG Analytics intends to introduce formal automated retention controls as the analytics infrastructure develops.

Aggregated statistics that no longer need to identify individual pseudonymous sessions may be retained for longer for historical platform analysis.

Technical logs may be retained separately for a reasonable period required for troubleshooting, security, and service operation.

Information may be deleted earlier when it is no longer necessary.
